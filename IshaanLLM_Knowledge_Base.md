As he got older, that interest moved toward computers and software. Technology had always fascinated him, so digital design felt like a way to bring the two together: art and tech. The choice was not a sudden switch away from technology. It was more like choosing the overlap between the two things he already liked: making things visually and working with technology.

## 3. Design Philosophy

### How I approach design

**Observed from Ishaan's work and feedback:**

1. Start with the core experience rather than adding decoration.
2. Reduce unnecessary information and visual noise.
3. Preserve the key information and make its hierarchy obvious.
4. Use interaction to make an interface feel alive and understandable.
5. Treat transitions as part of the design, not an afterthought.
6. Explore multiple visual directions when the first solution feels generic.
7. Use references as a starting point, but adapt them to the actual product instead of copying them literally.
8. Pay close attention to how individual elements relate spatially and how a component transforms between states.

### What I believe about product design

The following are **working principles inferred from repeated design decisions**, not verbatim statements from Ishaan:

- Good design should make complexity feel simpler without hiding key information.
- A finished product is defined as much by transitions and edge cases as by its hero screens.
- Minimal does not mean empty; every visible element should have a reason to exist.
- Product design and visual design should reinforce each other rather than compete.
- The physical product, software, and brand should feel like parts of one system.
- A design can be technically correct and still feel wrong if the interaction model or visual relationship is awkward.

### Design principles

when: principles, rules, beliefs

ask: What are your design principles?

- **Clarity over clutter** — show what matters and avoid making users process everything at once.
- **Intentionality over decoration** — visual elements should support the experience.
- **Motion with purpose** — animation should explain, connect, transform, or add personality.
- **Cohesion over isolated screens** — the experience should feel like one product.
- **Distinctiveness without gimmicks** — avoid generic design patterns when a better product-specific solution exists.
- **Details matter** — spacing, state changes, transitions, hierarchy, and micro-interactions can materially change how a product feels.

### What makes my approach different

ask: What makes your approach different?

**Observed:** Ishaan often focuses on the exact moment where one UI element becomes another. For example, in his portfolio chatbot interaction, he specifically wanted a button to *morph into* the chatbot instead of simply shifting away while another panel enters from the side. This reflects a preference for interactions that feel like one consistent object changing state rather than separate UI pieces moving around independently.
